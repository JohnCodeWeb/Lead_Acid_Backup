These lead acid batteries are very common in Zimbabwe, used for electric gates, electric fences and home generators. Growing up we would always replace these but keep the old ones in a shed. I remember connecting solar panels directly to the battery terminals and having them recharge enough to use a DC-AC converter like the one below. 

![image](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/cb289658-a6d8-4ff3-a113-c300968bc895)

Power outages were and still are very frequent in Zim, using this to charge my phone and PSP made me feel like iron man and if anything I would look forward to changing my batteries during the day so I could be prepared for the next time it went down. In 2014 we got our first Wifi modem and I was still able to use the same setup but it did not last long. I then moved on to some bigger car batteries but without the correct way to charge them, things never really went well and I think I ended up damaging the solar panels I had. 

I went back home recently and saw the electricity situation still hasn’t improved from when I left it and in fact things have gotten much harder. “A family of 4 people, on the other hand, reaches values between 8 and 9.5 kWh per day, which is approximately 262.5 kWh per month and 3,150 kWh per year.” This is not specific to Zimbabwe and was just the first result I saw on google. Backup power such as generators are common, however, reliant on fuel which is expensive and can sometimes also become scarce. Inverters and battery banks are also used in households that can afford the investment into the inverter unit, batteries, setup and maintenance. The batteries are charged while there is power, and the inverter switches when the power goes out. A step further would be the investment into solar panels and a controller unit but at 9.5kWh per day it gets expensive. 
For this project I would like to create an attachment that can use the old lead acid batteries back home so my younger brothers can make use of them. They don’t have to be old used batteries either as buying a new one is not too far of a reach in price. 

Some more examples are included later. The idea is the attachment is able to charge the battery through different inputs such as a DC barrel jack which may be common or cheap to purchase, USB-C connector which may now be the standard connector for electronics and can support up to 100 or even 240 watts. Lastly, a more optimistic implementation of connecting solar panels to charge the battery. I would also like to explore connecting it directly to a mains outlet as well.

You may ask why not use LiPos or 18650s ? It's not as available or affordable in ZIm
